A homemade astronomical observatory located on the outskirts of Orenburg, just 15 km from the city. Thanks to its distance from urban development, the observatory sits in an area with a Bortle scale light pollution level of class 5, making it a decent suburban spot for observing the night sky.

The observatory's main focus is deep-sky astrophotography - capturing objects such as nebulae, galaxies and star clusters. The equipment and technology used at the observatory make it possible to obtain high-quality images suitable for both scientific research and educational purposes.

The observatory operates in a semi-automatic mode, allowing it to be controlled remotely from anywhere in the world via the internet. This makes it accessible to amateur astronomers, researchers and enthusiasts worldwide.

## Observatory Equipment

The instruments and technology in use ensure high observation accuracy and professional image quality. Here's the main equipment used in our work:

- ### Mount: Sky-Watcher EQ6

    A reliable and precise mount that keeps the telescope stable even during long exposures.

- ### Telescope: Sky-Watcher BK2001P

    A reflector telescope with a 200 mm aperture, ideal for observing deep-sky objects.

- ### Main camera: ASI ZWO 6200MM Pro

    A highly sensitive astronomical camera with 24 MP resolution, capable of capturing the finest details of distant objects.

- ### Guide camera: QHY QHY5

    A camera for precise pointing and object tracking.

- ### Guide scope: SV106 Guide Scope 50mm (Helical Focuser)

    A compact telescope for guiding during long-exposure imaging.

- ### Focuser: ZWO EAF

    An electronic focuser that provides precise focus adjustment.

- ### Filter wheel: ZWO EFW 8x31mm

    A versatile filter wheel for working across different spectral ranges.

- ### Filters: ZWO L, R, G, B, Ha, OIII, SII (1.25")

    A set of filters for capturing color images and narrowband imaging.

- ### Coma corrector: Baader 2" Mark III MPCC

    Eliminates optical distortion, ensuring image sharpness.
