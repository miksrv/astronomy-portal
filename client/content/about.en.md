“Look at the Stars” is an amateur astronomy project from Orenburg: stargazing trips out of town, sidewalk astronomy evenings, our own observatory and an archive of astrophotos.

My name is [Misha](https://miksoft.pro). Everything you see on these pages grew out of one passion: nights at the telescope, lines of code and trips to the steppe with friends. Astronomy has long been a whole world for me: it gave me wonderful friends, taught me a lot and showed me how vast and mysterious the universe is. Here I gather everything we do together, so that space becomes closer and clearer to everyone.

## Our team

We work in very different fields, but we are all united by a love of the stars and the night sky. Since 2016 we have been telling people about astronomy, taking telescopes out of town and setting them up in the streets of Orenburg. Together we look at the stars, share what we know and inspire others.

## The “Look at the Stars” project

We started “Look at the Stars” in 2016 to make astronomy accessible to everyone. Since then, every season we give free lectures under the open sky, take people with telescopes far away from the city lights and hold sidewalk astronomy evenings right in the city.

Our main goal is to show how beautiful and vast the universe is and to inspire people to explore it. For the sake of a dark sky we travel to the towns and villages of the Orenburg region and to the neighbouring regions. The schedule and the archive of trips are on the [“Stargazing trips”](/stargazing) page, and if you want to look through a telescope without leaving the city, see [“Where to look through a telescope in Orenburg”](/stargazing/where).

A lecture is an important part of every trip. Experienced amateur astronomers give it: about scientific discoveries, the history of astronomy and what is happening in it today. We talk about complex things in simple words, so that listeners not only learn something new but want to keep going on their own.

We especially love sidewalk astronomy evenings: the telescopes stand in the busiest spots of the city, and any passer-by can come up, look at the Moon and the planets and ask what the night sky hides.

## The homemade observatory

The observatory is one of the most important chapters of my life and a rare thing for the Orenburg region. It works on its own and images deep-sky objects. How it is built and what equipment it runs on is told on the [“Observatory in Orenburg”](/observatory/overview) page.

The observatory's data is open to everyone: the [“Objects”](/objects) section holds an archive of FITS files you can use for studies, asteroid searches, astrophotography and other scientific and creative work. Finished images from the observatory and our trips are collected in the [“Astrophotos”](/photos) section, processed by me and my friends.

Soon we will open a free telescope control service, so that even more people can image the night sky themselves.

## Your help

The project has grown largely thanks to the “Native Towns” social investment programme of Gazprom Neft and the team of the Orenburg Media news agency.

The observatory has also found a response among many caring people. Our plans are big, and your support plays a key role in them. Thank you to everyone who helps with deeds, advice and consultations: thanks to you, space really is getting closer. If we have accidentally left someone out of the list, please write to us, it matters a lot.
